# Next landmark visual briefs

## Direction

The next exterior pass does not add six interchangeable futuristic buildings. It shows six different forms of contemporary power contaminating the public ground around them. Each location must communicate its joke before dialogue begins and must remain readable at the real 1.5 gameplay zoom.

The approved visual baseline is the physical integration established by the authority facades over the HD district plate: elevated front/orthographic projection, high-detail illustrated 2D game art, a clear bottom-centre entrance, a restrained palette, and a world patch that connects image, ground, entrance axis, and collision.

## Shared contract

- Give each location one dominant contradiction, readable at a glance.
- Express the contradiction with physical raster props and terrain scars, not abstract lines or painted route strips that resemble UI.
- Preserve a continuous approach corridor and an unmistakable interaction threshold.
- Keep the hero landmark denser than its apron; the ground supports the joke instead of becoming a prop catalogue.
- Match character scale, projection, pixel density, upper-left lighting, and the material wear of the current world plate.
- Allow asymmetry in small failures and repairs while retaining a strong overall silhouette.
- No readable labels are required to understand the premise.
- Bezos remains outside this pass: the drone and battle are an optional encounter and already have a working visual grammar.

## Six production briefs

### Putin — control under siege

The gilded ceremonial entrance remains absurdly immaculate while wartime continuity infrastructure consumes the forecourt. Concrete blast walls, sandbags, anti-tank obstacles, warning lamps, searchlights, cameras, and improvised cabling compress access into one suspiciously pristine central corridor. The joke is not simply “military”: it is a regime insisting on ceremony while visibly barricading itself from reality.

Prototype acceptance gate: the fortification must read at gameplay zoom, the centre route must remain physically and visually open, the player must not walk through the main barrier masses, and the added detail must feel attached to the district rather than pasted over it.

### Lagarde — stability moved downstairs

The upper tier presents calm stone, warm light, and protected access. The public level carries the cost: narrowed queue lanes, temporary austerity machinery, patched services, rolling shutters, receipts caught in drains, and maintenance that redirects inconvenience away from the immaculate VIP entrance. The joke is vertical and distributive: stability exists because instability has been assigned to someone else.

### Musk — the future in permanent beta

Keep the strong factory/launch silhouette, but make its unfinished present infect the apron. Exposed cable runs, mismatched prototype modules, temporary barriers, abandoned test fixtures, patched solar hardware, and conventional industrial repairs sit beneath a polished black-glass promise. The dominant contradiction is not generic futurism; it is a product launch permanently one update away from functioning.

### Xi — harmonious access control

Implemented as the complete north perimeter rather than a landmark placed inside the map. A continuous historic wall now meets both world edges; cameras, scanners, cable conduits and controlled lighting convert it into a literal administrative firewall. A Tiananmen-inspired red state gate sits on the exact central boulevard, with one open passage into `red_command`. The joke is frictionless harmony implemented as total observation.

### Kim Jong Un — supreme production value

Preserve the barbecue-cannon as the hero joke, but give it a theatrical military compound rather than leaving it floating at the map edge. Parade markings, painted viewing dais, floodlights, launch scaffolding, broadcast cameras, immaculate fake frontage, and visibly practical backstage cables turn strategic power into a televised stage show. It should feel dangerous, handmade, and very concerned with the camera angle.

### Sam Altman — aligned reactor demo

Reframe the nuclear plant as an inference reactor whose immaculate demonstration entrance hides improvised infrastructure. Fiber bundles feed old industrial pipes, redundant cooling units sit beside presentation lighting, emergency equipment remains packaged for investors, and the safe public route is clearly staged over a denser operational mess. The joke is confident alignment language powered by a system assembled faster than its consequences can be understood.
